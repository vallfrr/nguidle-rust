# Fidelity

Local replays cover selected input sequences, saves and game state. See [comparison notes](COMPARISON.md) for evidence against the original game.

## Differences waiting for the maintainer's validation (D3)

Nothing here is changed in the port yet. Items 1, 3 and 4 change nothing the player can see; item 2 changes the speed of one timer by about 5 %.

1. **Internal list capacities and version counters.** A save the port writes after a load is not byte for byte the save the original would write: some lists have less spare room (`Loadout.accessories` 10 slots against 16, `Card.manaCosts` 6 against 8) and different version counters. The game reads neither.
2. **Cooking banked time.** The timer is a float that grows by the frame time; at 40,000 s and more its step is 0.004 to 0.016 s, so its speed depends on the frame rate. The original runs about 6 % slow at 60 frames per second (and almost stands still in batch mode); the port, at 30 steps per second, runs about 5.5 % fast. See the recorded comparison below.
3. **Energy bar phase (ADR 0007).** The bar is up to a few hundredths of a bar apart at start-up; the counters stay equal after ten minutes.
4. **Interpreter shortcuts (D4).** Shortcuts 2, 5, 6, 7 and 20 answered nothing in any scenario; 3 and 21 are worth under 0.2 %. Removing them needs a late-game save on the console or your decision ([GUARDS.md](GUARDS.md)).

## Recorded comparisons

- The isolated Windows 1.260 copy reported Unity 2019.4.22f1. Its measured random vectors cover six seeds and 16 draws per seed for Unity values, integer and floating ranges, saved and restored states, and seeded Mono random methods. Run `cargo test -p clr --lib unity_random_tests` and `cargo test -p clr --lib dotnet_random::tests`. See [the probe](../tools/oracle/README.md). Further bounds, default seeding and gameplay uses remain open.
- In three isolated fresh-game runs at approximately 60 seconds of wall time, the original gained 58 energy, from 250 to 308, with a 500 cap. Steam initialization and autosave updates were disabled in the copied executable for the probe. The port interpreter's 1,800 fixed steps likewise gained 58 energy. The initial bar phases differed, so these runs do not prove identical bar animation or full progression. The [probe](../tools/oracle/README.md) describes the setup.
- The first full guarded replay produced differing Cooking text while the menu was parked off-screen. That skip was removed and a targeted game-data test added. Performance impact on the Vita has not been measured.
- With the same copied save loaded into the original and interpreter, both started at 500/500 energy and 12,934 total gained, and reached 12,992 gained after respectively 60.00114 and 60.000221 seconds of real time. The interpreter was paced at 30 ticks per second. The energy bar began at 0.04 and ended at 0.9399996 in the original versus 0.8799997 in the interpreter. The initial state and the first 100 updates matched when aligned by callback count. The original ran its first three callbacks within 10 ms of observation, before the interpreter's first simulated callback. [ADR 0007](adr/0007-progress-bar-phase.md) accepts this observed startup timing difference for the measured baseline.
- `just verify-fixtures` compares 60 synthetic cases between .NET 8 and the interpreter. The suite covers control flow, seeded random draws, exception handling, captured lambdas, generic lists, LINQ summation, iterator state machines, and structures with properties, copies, nested fields, arrays, boxing and by-reference and output parameters. Each case covers a specific construction and does not establish general support for it.

- Offline progress, 27 November 2026 (local, with `tools/oracle/OfflineProbe.cs` and `compare_saves.py`): the original and the port opened the same two saves, written by the port, one day (all energy in attack training, every feature unlocked) and three hours (the maintainer's early PC save) after they were made. Right after the load, all 7,316 and 7,146 fields of the resulting saves were equal (numbers within 1e-6), apart from the version counters and spare capacity of internal lists. After 60 seconds of real time the fields that differ are timers and the training bar phase (ADR 0007), about 0.05 second apart, and `lastTime`, which the probe leaves alone because it skips autosave.
- The same long game played with and without the interpreter shortcuts (`tools/longrun.sh`: 9 rounds of 150 pseudo-random clicks and 20 minutes of game time, two hours of offline progress in the middle, from the maintainer's early PC save, fixed clock) ended in byte-identical saves (124,460 bytes). The run without shortcuts took 343 seconds against 97 seconds.
- The full-game script (`just full-game`) gives the same numbers natively and as WebAssembly (the web build): for example attack 125,971,416.03207271 after one game hour of simple training in both.
- Offline progress on a state of eight game hours (7,815 fields, absences of 28 hours and of more than a year, which the game caps), 27 November 2026: one field differed, the beast-quest timer, because the original truncates `(int)((float)22560 * 0.9f)` to 20,303 and the port rounded to 20,304. Fixed ([ADR 0014](adr/0014-float-arithmetic-before-integer-conversion.md)); all 7,815 fields now match.
- Offline progress, five more played states (three from a new game with every feature unlocked, two from the maintainer's early save, each after 2 game hours of pseudo-random clicks, then ten hours of absence): after the load, two states matched on every field and three differed only by the card spawn timer (0.0004 to 0.002 s, one frame) and by internal list capacities.
- Internal list capacities: after a load the original's lists have more spare room than the port's (`Loadout.accessories` 16 slots against 10, `Card.manaCosts` 8 against 6) and their version counters differ. The game never reads these values, but a save written by the port after a load is not byte for byte the save the original would write. Not changed; D3 asks the maintainer to validate it.
- Ten minutes of real time after opening a played state (7,704 fields, two states, 5 and 10 minutes): all fields match except eight. Five timers are about 0.06 s apart (the original runs on real frames, the port on 30 steps per second). The energy bar phase differs (0.02 against 0.98 of a bar, ADR 0007), while the energy gained and every other counter are equal after 10 minutes: the offset does not build up. `dailySaveRewardTime` only moves in the original's autosave routine, which the probe disables. `cooking.cookTimer`: see the next item.
- Cooking banked time (`cooking.cookTimer`, a float that holds up to 172,800 s) grows by `+= Time.deltaTime` each frame. A float that large has a step of 0.004 to 0.016 s, so the result depends on the frame time: in the original in batch mode (very short frames) the timer barely moved (+1.7 s in 5 minutes), at 60 frames per second it runs about 6 % slow, and in the port at 30 steps per second it runs about 5.5 % fast (+316 s in 300 s). This is the original game's own rounding; it cannot be identical for every frame rate. Not changed; D3 asks the maintainer to validate it.
- All 58 menus open on the early save and on a played state: the original raises nothing, the port raised twice until [ADR 0015](adr/0015-get-component-in-children-and-inactive-objects.md).

## Unresolved intermittent save difference

An earlier report recorded one differing complete save after an invocation change, followed by passing runs, without retaining the differing files. The four current guard and locale combinations passed, but that cannot establish the cause of the historical result. The comparison remains enabled; a future failure must retain both saves, inputs, seeds, logs and environment for investigation. This issue remains open rather than being declared fixed by retries.
